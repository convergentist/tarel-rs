# W-GAN Research Line — Three Sub-Proposals Built on Sub-Proposal 1

The W-GAN architecture specified is a near-perfect fit for the recurrence-plot framework: the 64×64 grayscale output is exactly the shape of a Distance Plot. This is not a coincidence to be hidden in the paper — it should be the *opening move*. The DP is naturally a single-channel 2D image with continuous values; Tanh-bounded outputs are appropriate after normalization to [-1, 1]; and the fixed event-time windowing scheme with N=64 yields DPs that are *natively* 64×64, matching the architecture without resampling. The other two clocks (60-sec physical and variable info-time) produce variable-length DPs that must be resampled to 64×64 — a design choice that itself becomes a methodological discussion in §4 of the paper. Recommendation: bicubic interpolation for the DP and nearest-neighbor for the RP, with both choices ablated to show robustness.

The three sub-proposals below use this W-GAN as the empirical engine for testing the clock-effect hypotheses from the parent sub-proposal 1.

## Sub-proposal 1.A — Clock as Implicit Regularizer of the Saddle-Point Optimization

**Headline hypothesis.** A stochastic clock is not merely a representation choice over the data — it is an implicit regularizer of the data distribution that propagates to the W-GAN's saddle-point dynamics. Concretely, information-time data, being more stationary in its conditional moments, induces a min-max objective whose Lipschitz landscape is gentler, whose gradient-penalty term is better-behaved, and whose practical convergence (not asymptotic — there are no guarantees there) is more reliable.

**Why this matters.** Alternating SGD on a non-convex-non-concave minimax has no convergence guarantees, and practical stability is largely empirical art. If the choice of clock measurably improves training stability under fixed seed, fixed architecture, and fixed hyperparameters, the result connects two literatures — microstructure subordination and GAN training dynamics — that have never spoken to each other. This is exactly the kind of "obvious in hindsight" claim that travels well at NeurIPS.

**Experimental protocol.** Train identical W-GAN-GPs on three DP datasets generated under each of the three windowing schemes. Hold everything else fixed: 128-dim latent, 5:1 critic:generator update ratio, Adam with β1=0 and β2=0.9, λ_GP=10, batch size 64, thirty random seeds per condition. The instrumentation matters more than the architecture: track the EM-gap proxy as the critic's score-difference between real and fake batches across training, smoothed over 100-step windows — the literature reads this as a Wasserstein-1 estimate, and the headline plot is its variance across seeds by clock. Track the per-step gradient-penalty magnitude (||∇_x̂ D(x̂)|| − 1)² and report its full empirical distribution per clock; information-time should produce a tighter distribution because the data manifold has more uniform local geometry, and that prediction is testable. Track mode coverage by generating 10k samples at fixed checkpoints and running reverse nearest-neighbor search against the training set to count unique near-matches — mode collapse is the central failure mode of GANs and the claim is that information-time reduces its incidence. Finally, track critic-loss curvature along the realized generator update direction via finite-difference second-order — this is the more involved diagnostic but it is the right one for a genuinely saddle-point-geometric claim rather than a vague stability claim.

**Expected result and risk.** If information-time wins on at least three of the four diagnostics across seeds, that's a clean publishable result. The risk is that the effect could be small or noise-dominated; the mitigation is to run thirty seeds (cheap with this architecture) and report bootstrap confidence intervals rather than point estimates. The Rust implementation is the edge here — substantially more seeds than a PyTorch researcher in the same wallclock — and the paper should say so explicitly with throughput numbers in the appendix, since reviewers respond to demonstrated rigor under tight compute.

## Sub-proposal 1.B — Generative Fidelity as a Joint Test of Clock Choice and Model Capacity

**Headline hypothesis.** When the W-GAN is trained on DPs from each clock, the gap between the RQA-statistical distribution of real DPs and the RQA-statistical distribution of generated DPs is smallest under information-time. In words: the same architecture applied to data from a better clock produces samples that are closer to the real manifold *as measured by the same RQA suite that motivated the clock change in the first place*.

**Why this is the most rhetorically efficient experiment in the paper.** It closes the loop. The parent sub-proposal 1 used RQA distributions to argue that information-time data is more stationary. Here we use RQA distributions on *generated* data to ask whether that improved stationarity translates into improved generative fidelity. Same instrument, two roles, one consistent conclusion if it works. Reviewers will read the methodological coherence as a sign of careful thought, which it is.

**Experimental protocol.** Train one W-GAN per clock — or, equivalently and more parameter-efficiently, one clock-conditional W-GAN with a 3-token clock embedding modulating an early FiLM layer. The conditional is the right call given saddle-point fragility, since training one model with conditioning is dramatically more stable than training three independent models with the variance-across-seeds problem. Generate 10k synthetic DPs per clock. Compute the six RQA scalars (RR, DET, LAM, TT, L_max, ENTR) on real-train, real-test, and synthetic populations. For each scalar, report sliced Wasserstein distance between (real-test, real-train) — the irreducible empirical noise floor — and (real-test, synthetic) — the model's gap. The headline finding is that the gap-to-noise-floor ratio is smallest under information-time.

**Two extensions worth doing.** First, train W-GANs of varying capacity by scaling generator and critic channel widths by 0.5, 1, and 2, and trace the fidelity-capacity curve per clock. The hypothesis is that information-time saturates capacity earlier, which is exactly the data-efficiency claim from the parent proposal but cast in capacity rather than data terms — and the two framings should give consistent answers, providing internal triangulation. Second, evaluate the *critic itself* as a fidelity metric: its Wasserstein-1 estimate on held-out real-vs-synthetic batches is essentially a learned divergence, and reporting it alongside RQA distance gives reviewers a non-circular triangulation between the model's internal scoring and the external RQA-based scoring.

**Risk.** RQA on generated DPs is meaningful only if the generator hasn't memorized the training set. Add a duplication detector (per-pixel L2 to nearest training DP) and report the empirical CDF of nearest-neighbor distances; if it's too tight, the model is memorizing and the RQA-fidelity numbers are uninformative. Memorization in W-GANs is rare relative to autoregressive models, but the test is cheap and reviewers will ask for it.

## Sub-proposal 1.C — Conditional W-GAN over (Clock × Exchange × Symbol): Toward a Foundation DP Generator

**Headline hypothesis.** A single conditional W-GAN trained over all 36 regimes (3 clocks × 3 exchanges × 4 symbols) learns a latent space that disentangles clock effects, exchange effects, and symbol effects, and can generate plausible DPs for arbitrary (clock, exchange, symbol) tuples — including held-out ones — via conditional interpolation. This is the foundation-model framing applied to recurrence geometry.

**Why this is the contribution-bearing sub-proposal.** Single-regime W-GAN training is well-trodden; what makes the work novel for NeurIPS is the *cross-regime structure* the conditional model exposes. The bps + volume-scaling pipeline (Layer 2) produces DPs that are dimensionally and statistically comparable across symbols — without that normalization, joint training would be incoherent. This sub-proposal is therefore also the empirical validation of the entire Layer 2 pipeline as a microstructure-normalization scheme, which is itself a publishable claim independent of the W-GAN result.

**Conditioning architecture.** Extend the generator with FiLM (Feature-wise Linear Modulation) layers after each BatchNorm: per-layer affine parameters γ, β are predicted from the concatenation of (clock_emb_3, exchange_emb_8, symbol_emb_8) by a tiny MLP. The critic receives the same condition vector concatenated as additional channels at the input, broadcast across spatial dimensions (the standard "channel concat" trick for conditional critics). FiLM is the right call over naïve concat in the generator because it preserves the residual structure of BN + ReLU and adds well under 1% to the parameter count. In the tch-rs stack this is a clean implementation against the existing nn::SequentialT skeleton — the FiLM block becomes a custom Module that takes (x, cond) and returns x * γ(cond) + β(cond), and slots in between each existing BN and ReLU.

**Three experiments that earn the "foundation model" framing.** First, *disentanglement via linear probes*: after training, fit linear classifiers on the critic's penultimate features to recover (clock, exchange, symbol) labels independently, and on intermediate generator activations under matched conditioning. A successful disentanglement story is that clock identity is linearly recoverable from early layers while exchange and symbol identities are only recoverable from later layers — meaning the model has learned a hierarchy of microstructural abstraction, with clock-induced geometry as the most basic feature. Second, *leave-one-out cross-regime generation*: hold out one (exchange, symbol) pair entirely from training, then generate DPs for the held-out pair by conditioning on its embedding (initialized as the average of nearby embeddings, then optionally fine-tuned for a few hundred steps). Score with the RQA fidelity from sub-proposal 1.B. The headline number is the gap between held-out fidelity and in-distribution fidelity; a small gap validates that the model has learned transferable microstructural priors rather than just memorized per-symbol idiosyncrasies. Third, *counterfactual clock generation*: take a real DP from (Binance, BTC, physical-clock) and generate its counterfactual under (Binance, BTC, info-time-clock) by holding the latent z fixed and changing only the clock conditioning. If the latent encodes content and the conditioning encodes clock-induced geometry, this should produce systematic, interpretable shifts in the DP topology — and via the RQA suite, a directly measurable shift in stylized facts. This is the experiment that most directly probes the Clark / Mandelbrot-Taylor subordination hypothesis inside a learned generative model, and to my knowledge it has not been done before.

**Atelier consequence.** A trained conditional generator becomes a drop-in synthetic-data engine for any (exchange, symbol, clock) tuple Atelier needs. New symbols added to the platform require only a Layer 2 pipeline run plus a learned embedding; the generator transfers without retraining. This is the concrete practical justification for the cross-asset training move and the kind of artifact-as-contribution that sets up follow-on work.

## How the three fit together

These sub-proposals are designed to be performed in sequence on the same codebase. Sub-proposal 1.A establishes that the W-GAN *trains better* under information-time. Sub-proposal 1.B establishes that it *generates better* under information-time. Sub-proposal 1.C establishes that it *generalizes better* across regimes when trained jointly. The narrative arc — stability, then fidelity, then generalization — is the standard ML-paper progression, and reviewers will read it without friction.

Two implementation observations specific to the tch-rs stack. First, the per-sample gradient penalty forbids BN in the critic; once FiLM-conditioned generators are introduced in 1.C, the generator's BN should likely be swapped for GroupNorm or LayerNorm, because BN's batch-coupling interacts poorly with conditional training when batches contain a mix of regimes — generator stability degrades in ways that look like clock-effect noise but aren't. Second, the 5:1 critic:generator ratio is the W-GAN-GP default but worth ablating to 1:1 and 10:1 in sub-proposal 1.A, since the saddle-point-stability claim is only meaningful relative to a deliberately tested hyperparameter neighborhood — reviewers will check this and a clean ablation table preempts the objection.

A final framing note. The paper's central rhetorical move is to take a finance-microstructure observation (information-time produces more Gaussian conditional distributions) and operationalize it as a measurable improvement in modern deep-generative training dynamics. That bridge — from Clark 1973 to W-GAN-GP saddle points — is the result that NeurIPS reviewers will actually find novel. §1 of the paper should foreground it explicitly and not bury the lede under microstructure background.
