\blockhead{ioEdge}{Block 11 --- Wasserstein score
$D_\phi(\mathbf{x}) \in \mathbb{R}$}

The output is a single real number per input image, unbounded in
either direction. Its absolute scale carries no information --- only
the \emph{difference} between average scores of real and fake batches
matters --- so reading individual scores in isolation is not
meaningful. The two ways the score is consumed during training are: (i)
the Earth-Mover-gap diagnostic, which tracks
$\mathbb{E}_{\text{real}}[D_\phi] - \mathbb{E}_{\text{fake}}[D_\phi]$
per critic step and feeds the final-100-step mean and standard
deviation into the four-axis Stability Score; (ii) the gradient-penalty
loss, which differentiates $D_\phi$ with respect to interpolants
$\hat{\mathbf{x}} = \alpha \mathbf{x}_{\mathrm{real}} + (1 - \alpha)
G_\theta(z)$ and penalises departures from $\|\nabla_{\hat{\mathbf{x}}}
D_\phi(\hat{\mathbf{x}})\|_2 = 1$. Both consumers use the raw scalar
output without any further normalisation, so $D_\phi$'s final stage is
the literal arithmetic dual of the generator's terminal $\tanh$: the
generator squashes onto $[-1, 1]$; the critic emits onto $(-\infty,
+\infty)$.
