\section{Methods}
\label{sec:methods}

For each $(\text{instrument}, \text{seed}, \text{clock})$ triple a
fresh W-GAN-GP is trained on the normalized $64 \times 64$
recurrence-plot tensors of that (instrument, clock) for a fixed
wallclock budget (default $T_{\text{budget}} = 20$ minutes per cell,
overridable via \texttt{--budget-mins}). The generator is a
five-stage transposed-convolution stack with BatchNorm and ReLU
activations terminating in a $\tanh$ to bound output to $[-1, 1]$,
mapping $z \in \mathbb{R}^{128}$ to a $1 \times 64 \times 64$ image.
The critic is a five-stage stride-2 convolution stack with
LeakyReLU(0.2) terminating in a scalar; no BatchNorm in the critic,
since cross-sample correlations interfere with the per-sample
gradient penalty. Locked hyperparameters are Adam ($\beta_1 = 0$,
$\beta_2 = 0.9$), learning rate $10^{-4}$, $\lambda_{\mathrm{GP}} =
10$, batch size $64$, $n_{\text{critic}} = 5$.

The data loader samples training tensors uniformly with replacement
from the per-(cell, clock) recurrence-plot bank persisted by Stage~1;
no train/validation split is taken and every admitted bucket is
eligible for every batch. Checkpoints are written every $1{,}000$
generator steps; the locked specification dictates that the
mode-coverage and critic-loss-curvature axes of the Stability Score
are sampled only at checkpoint boundaries, so the first axis values
become available at the first checkpoint and stabilise as the
training horizon extends. The present run reaches the first
checkpoint at step $1{,}000$ for the trained pairs, populating the
EM-gap and GP-magnitude axes for the entire trajectory but leaving
the other two axes empty by construction.

\subsection*{Generator}
\begin{center}
\scalebox{0.75}{\input{content/figures/generator/generator-architecture}}
\end{center}

The generator is a five-stage transposed-convolution stack. Stage~1
lifts the latent vector into a high-channel $4 \times 4$ spatial grid
via a kernel-4, stride-1 transposed convolution with zero padding;
Stages~2--4 each double the spatial resolution and halve the channel
count via kernel-4, stride-2, padding-1 transposed convolutions
(canonical DCGAN upsampling); Stage~5 collapses the 64-channel
feature map to one channel and replaces $\mathrm{BN} \circ
\mathrm{ReLU}$ with $\tanh$, bounding the per-pixel output to
$(-1, 1)$. The $\tanh$ choice is consistent with the per-bucket
min--max normalisation applied to the training tensors, which scales
every training recurrence plot to $[-1, 1]$ via an invertible
$(\min, \max)$-tuple. Because the generator's output is bounded by the
same interval as the training data, the per-pixel histograms of
$G_\theta(z)$ and a training tensor live on the same support, which
is a precondition for the Wasserstein-1 distance estimator to be
well-defined under the W-GAN-GP duality.

Roughly $3.8$~M learnable parameters in total; Stage~2 dominates the
budget because both its input and output channel counts are large;
stage~5 is essentially free. This concentration of capacity in the
middle layers is intentional: the early-middle stages of a
transposed-convolutional generator are where the bulk of
distributional structure is committed, while the late stages mainly
perform geometric refinement.

\subsection*{Critic}
\begin{center}
\scalebox{0.75}{\input{content/figures/critic/critic-architecture}}
\end{center}

The critic $D_\phi$ is the topological dual of the generator: it
consumes a $1 \times 64 \times 64$ image (either a real training
tensor or a fake $G_\theta(z)$) and emits a single real-valued score
whose difference of expectations over $P_r$ and $P_g$ approximates the
Wasserstein-1 distance $W_1(P_r, P_g)$. The architecture mirrors the
generator's doubling pattern: spatial resolution is \emph{halved} at
every stage. The terminal stage uses a kernel-4, stride-1 convolution
with no activation, producing the scalar Wasserstein score. The
gradient-penalty objective regularises $\|\nabla_{\hat{x}}
D_\phi(\hat{x})\|_2 \to 1$ at random interpolants
$\hat{x} = \alpha x_{\text{real}} + (1-\alpha) G_\theta(z)$, enforcing
the Lipschitz constraint that licenses the dual estimator.

\subsection*{Diagnostics}
At each critic step we record (i) the EM-gap
$\mathbb{E}_{\text{real}}[D_\phi] -
\mathbb{E}_{\text{fake}}[D_\phi]$, with a 100-step rolling smooth and
the final-100-step mean and standard deviation; (ii) the
gradient-penalty magnitude $(\|\nabla_{\hat{x}} D_\phi(\hat{x})\|_2 -
1)^2$ per critic step. At each $5{,}000$-generator-step checkpoint we
additionally record (iii) the mode-coverage count, computed by drawing
$10{,}000$ samples from $G_\theta$ and counting those whose nearest
training-set $L_2$ neighbour distance is at most the median
per-instrument training-pairwise $L_2$; and (iv) a critic-loss
curvature estimate from a three-point finite difference of the
generator loss along the last gradient direction. The four scalars
form the four axes of the locked Stability Score
(\texttt{StabilityScoreSpec\_v0\_1\_0.json}) under
rank-normalisation across the seed population.

\subsection*{Hardware and runtime}
Training was performed on a single NVIDIA GeForce GTX 1080~Ti
(Pascal, \texttt{sm\_61}, $11$ GiB VRAM) under Ubuntu with NVIDIA
driver $580.142$ and CUDA $12.4$. Because Pascal-class GPUs were
dropped from the default \texttt{PyTorch} CUDA wheel binaries
beginning with \texttt{PyTorch}~2.6, the runs reported here use a
pinned \texttt{PyTorch}~2.5.1 build with \texttt{sm\_61} support and
\texttt{tch-rs} bindings for the Stage-2 trainer. The cuDNN benchmark
flag is disabled at process start as part of the determinism contract,
forcing deterministic convolution-algorithm selection at the cost of
some throughput. The mode-coverage nearest-neighbour search is tiled
to keep peak VRAM under $2$ GiB ($1{,}000$ rows per tile); other
allocations during training fit comfortably within the $11$ GiB budget
at the locked batch size of $64$ and the $64{\times}64$ tensor shape.
Per-(cell, clock) wallclock at the first checkpoint
(Table~\ref{tab:stage_two_summary}) is approximately $370$ seconds,
i.e.\ roughly $2.7$ critic steps per second and $0.54$ generator
steps per second on the present hardware.
